Every year a handful of small towns quietly become "the" small town everyone's talking about — a chef opens somewhere unexpected, a remote-work migration reshapes a downtown, or a town just finally gets the infrastructure to support the life people already wanted to live there. Here's what's been coming up lately.

**Bentonville, Arkansas.** A corporate headquarters turned an entire region into one of the best mountain-biking trail networks in the country, almost by accident. The art museum helped too.

**Paducah, Kentucky.** A UNESCO Creative City designation and a genuinely inexpensive downtown have been quietly pulling artists and makers away from pricier scenes for years now.

**Greenville, South Carolina.** Technically past "small town" by population, but its downtown core still runs on small-town logic — walkable, dense with independent restaurants, anchored by a river park that used to be a highway overpass.

**Hood River, Oregon.** Already on our [foodie guide](/guides/best-small-towns-for-foodies/), but worth flagging again — the Gorge's wind-sport and cidery scene keeps compounding on itself.

**Beaufort, North Carolina** (not to be confused with Beaufort, South Carolina). A working waterfront town that's stayed unusually unpretentious for how photogenic it is.

We'll keep this list updated as new towns earn their moment — if you think we're missing an obvious one, that's the kind of thing a future update to this post is for.
