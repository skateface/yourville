The single biggest mistake we hear about after the fact isn't picking the wrong town — it's picking a town based on the wrong visit. A great weekend trip and a livable town are not the same claim, and most vacations are engineered, on purpose, to make a place look better than it will feel on a random Tuesday in February.

A few adjustments that actually close the gap:

**Go on a bad-weather day.** Anyone can love a town in perfect sun. Visit during the season you're least excited about, not the one on the postcard.

**Do your actual errands there.** Find the grocery store, the pharmacy, the hardware store. A town's charm often lives downtown, but your life will mostly happen at the edges of it.

**Talk to someone who moved there in the last two years**, not someone who's lived there for thirty. Recent transplants remember the friction you're about to experience; longtime locals have forgotten it exists.

**Sit in on something recurring** — a farmers market, a town council meeting, a Sunday service. One-off events are curated. Recurring ones show you the town's actual rhythm.

**Price groceries and services, not just real estate.** Housing costs get all the research energy; the weekly cost of living is what you'll actually feel.

None of this replaces the [quiz](/quiz/) or the [guides](/guides/) for narrowing down *which kind* of town fits your life. But once you've got a shortlist, how you visit matters almost as much as where.
